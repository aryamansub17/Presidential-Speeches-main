PROJECT TITLE: Presidential speech program
PURPOSE OF PROJECT: Analyze trends in presidential speeches
VERSION or DATE: Oct 15, 2026
HOW TO START THIS PROJECT: 
AUTHORS: Aryaman Subramanian
USER INSTRUCTIONS: 


For the early era I used George Washington (1789) and Thomas Jefferson (1801). For the middle era
I used Abraham Lincoln (1861) and Theodore Roosevelt (1905). For the modern
era I used John F. Kennedy (1961) and Barack Obama (2009).

1. Speech Length and Word Complexity

The older speeches were not really longer or shorter than the modern ones.
Washington had 1444 words and Jefferson had 1723, which is close to Kennedy
with 1365 and Obama with 2402. The longest speech was Lincoln's with 3618
words, and the shortest was Roosevelt's with 910 words. I think Lincoln's was
so long because the country was about to go to war and he had a lot to
explain. The words did get shorter over time though. Washington's average
word length was 4.92 and Jefferson's was 4.76, but Kennedy and Obama were
both about 4.46. The older speeches used big fancy words that people don't
really use anymore.

2. Target Word Frequencies

Not all four words showed up in every speech. "People" and "our" were in all
six, but "government" was not in Roosevelt's or Kennedy's speech, and
"united" was not in Jefferson's or Roosevelt's. In the early speeches,
"government" was used the most out of the target words. In the modern
speeches, "our" was used way more than anything else. Obama said "our" 67
times, while Washington only said it once. I think this happened because
the early presidents were still explaining how the new government worked.
Modern presidents want to connect with normal people, so they say "our" a
lot to make everyone feel like they are part of it.

3. My Thoughts

Overall, I think presidential speeches changed from being formal and about
the government to being simpler and more about the people. The words got
shorter and "our" got way more popular. This might be because modern
speeches are on TV and the internet for everyone to hear, so they need to be
easy to understand. One problem with my program is that it counts
"self-government" as one word, so Roosevelt shows 0 for "government" even
though he talks about self-government. Also, I only looked at six speeches,
so I can't say for sure that these patterns are true for every speech.
